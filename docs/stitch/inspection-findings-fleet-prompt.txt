ROAM FLEET — INSPECTION FINDINGS ("FOUND, NOT DONE YET") — FULL DESIGN PROMPT (GOOGLE STITCH)




Roam Fleet - Log service · What the inspection found (Desktop Web App with a phone layout, for fleet owners, fleet managers and their staff):

Design the screens that let a fleet manager record what a garage inspection found on a car, and keep track of the jobs that weren't done yet. Often the fleet pays only for an inspection ("Inspection / diagnostic fee", J$8,000) to find out what the car needs. Today the existing "Log service" dialog of Fleet maintenance can only record that fee. Now, when the job is an inspection, the Details step gets a new section "What the inspection found". The person adds each job the garage said the car needs, picking from Roam's normal list of jobs, and marks each one "Done today" or "Not done yet". A "Done today" job is part of this service, with its own cost and parts. A "Not done yet" job costs J$0, can carry a short note ("rear left leaking"), and is kept on the car's list "Found by inspection". Later, from that list, each waiting job is closed in one of two ways: "Log it now", which opens Log service with that job already picked, or "Not needed", which removes it from the list but keeps it in history. The fleet page shows how many found jobs are waiting on each car. The dialog keeps its four steps: 1 Date & mileage, 2 Work done, 3 Details, 4 Review. The person using this is a normal fleet owner, not a mechanic: plain words, no codes. The design must feel modern, calm and effortless, like the best business banking apps. Draw each screen tall enough that every element is visible, with no inner scrolling and nothing cut off.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES:
- Capture, never block: the person can add found jobs or skip the section. Nothing is ticked for them.
- The money adds up in front of them: "Not done yet" jobs always show "J$ 0 · not charged", and the bill only counts the inspection and the Done-today jobs.
- One list, one place: everything the inspection found lives with the car under "Found by inspection" until it's done or marked not needed.
- Every found job always shows three things: what it is, when it was found and by which inspection, and the button(s) that close it.
- Nothing disappears silently: closed jobs stay in the inspection's history with their status.
- Add to the existing screens, never take anything away: every part of today's Log service dialog, Due items drawer and fleet page stays as it is.
- Calm, not alarming: amber for waiting, red only for Overdue, errors and Remove.
- Desktop-first (1440px), with a phone layout. 8px spacing grid, hairline borders, soft shadows, skeleton loading.
- One view per screen.

INTERACTIONS:
- "What the inspection found" appears only on inspection jobs (like "Inspection / diagnostic fee", "Leak Inspection", "Exhaust System Inspection"); other jobs look exactly as they do today.
- "+ Add job" opens the Screen 2 overlay; "Add" puts the job in the list with "Not done yet" selected and the overlay closes. A job already in the list shows "Added" instead of the button.
- Switching a job to "Done today" hides its note and "J$ 0"; after "Save line" on the inspection, the Done-today job opens as its own job card (Screen 4). Switching back to "Not done yet" removes that job card.
- "Remove" takes the job off the list at once, with an "Undo" button inside the toast.
- "Save service" saves everything once; the button shows a spinner and can't be pressed twice.
- "N to do" chip and the "FOUND BY INSPECTION" card open the Due items drawer of that car (the card opens the car with the oldest waiting job).
- "Log it now" closes the drawer and opens Log service on step 3 with the job picked and the banner (Screen 10). Saving it moves the job out of "Found by inspection" and marks it "Done" in the inspection's history.
- "Not needed" opens the Screen 9 overlay; "Mark not needed" closes the job, shows the toast and updates the counts at once.
- If someone else closed the job first, the action is refused politely (Screen 12 amber state), the list refreshes, nothing is lost.
- On the phone, the buttons are full width and the drawer is a full-screen sheet.
